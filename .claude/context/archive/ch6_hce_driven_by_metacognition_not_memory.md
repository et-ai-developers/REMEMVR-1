### Key Finding from RQ 6.6.2

**Dunning-Kruger Effect REJECTED in VR Episodic Memory**

**Cognitive Factor (Memory Ability):**
- Predictor: z_baseline_accuracy (IRT theta at Test 1)
- Effect on HCE: β = -0.001, SE = 0.002, p_bonf = 1.000
- Interpretation: Memory ability has ZERO relationship with HCE tendency
- Correlation: r = -0.04 (essentially no correlation)
- **Conclusion:** Low performers are NOT more prone to HCEs

**Metacognitive Factors (Confidence Miscalibration):**

1. **Confidence Bias (Overconfidence):**
   - Predictor: z_confidence_bias (conf_theta - acc_theta at Test 1)
   - Effect on HCE: β = +0.010, SE = 0.002, p_bonf < .001
   - Interpretation: Overconfident individuals make more HCEs
   - **Strong positive predictor**

2. **Baseline Confidence:**
   - Predictor: z_baseline_confidence (confidence theta at Test 1)
   - Effect on HCE: β = +0.009, SE = 0.002, p_bonf < .001
   - Interpretation: High confidence → more HCEs (OPPOSITE of hypothesis)
   - Hypothesis predicted: High confidence = good self-knowledge → fewer HCEs
   - Finding: High confidence may reflect OVERCONFIDENCE at encoding
   - **Moderate correlation with accuracy:** r = 0.57, but accuracy predicts NOTHING

### Theoretical Implications

**Fleming & Lau (2014) Two-Dimensional Model:**
- **Dimension 1 (Cognitive):** Memory performance (Type 1 task)
- **Dimension 2 (Metacognitive):** Confidence accuracy (Type 2 task)
- **Critical insight:** These dimensions are SEPARABLE
- **VR finding confirms:** Memory ability ≠ metacognitive monitoring quality

**Challenge to Dunning-Kruger Effect:**
- **Classic D-K:** Low performers have poor self-insight (metacognitive deficit)
- **VR finding:** Low accuracy does NOT predict poor metacognition
- **Implication:** D-K may NOT generalize to VR episodic memory domain
- **Alternative:** HCEs driven by overconfidence trait, not ability deficit

### Model Fit

- **Conclusion:** Metacognition drives HCE, not memory ability

### Unexpected Direction: Baseline Confidence

**Hypothesis:** High confidence = good self-knowledge → FEWER HCEs
**Finding:** High confidence → MORE HCEs (β = +0.009, p < .001)

**Possible Explanations:**
1. **Overconfidence at encoding:** High baseline confidence reflects trait overconfidence, not accurate self-assessment
2. **Confidence-accuracy dissociation:** Confidence and accuracy correlate (r = 0.57) but confidence predicts HCEs while accuracy does NOT
3. **Metacognitive style:** Some individuals are consistently high-confidence regardless of actual performance
4. **VR-specific effect:** Immersive VR may inflate confidence independently of accuracy

**Implication:** Baseline confidence is NOT a proxy for metacognitive quality in VR

### Practical Implications

**Who makes HCEs?**
- NOT low performers (memory ability irrelevant)
- Overconfident individuals (confidence_bias predicts HCEs)
- High baseline confidence individuals (may reflect overconfidence trait)

**Clinical/Educational Applications:**
- Cannot identify HCE-prone individuals via memory tests alone
- Must assess metacognitive calibration directly
- Overconfidence is targetable via metacognitive training
- VR assessment reveals metacognitive style independent of ability

### Summary

HCEs in VR episodic memory are driven by METACOGNITIVE factors (overconfidence, confidence miscalibration), NOT cognitive factors (memory ability). This challenges Dunning-Kruger generalization to VR domain and supports Fleming & Lau's two-dimensional model. Memory performance and metacognitive monitoring are separable dimensions. Low performers are NOT worse at metacognition. Instead, overconfident individuals (regardless of ability level) are prone to HCEs. This has important theoretical (D-K boundary conditions) and practical (metacognitive training targets) implications.
