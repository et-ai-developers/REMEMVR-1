# RQ 6.3.2 - When Domain Metacognitive Paradox

## When Domain Paradox - Metacognitive Dynamics (2025-12-11 21:45)

**Finding:** When domain shows OPPOSITE calibration trajectory (overconfident→underconfident) compared to What/Where domains (underconfident→overconfident).

### The Paradox

**When domain (temporal memory) trajectory:**
- **Early (T1):** OVERCONFIDENT (+0.377) despite floor-effect accuracy
- **Late (T4):** UNDERCONFIDENT (-0.351)
- **Change:** Δ = -0.727 (IMPROVING calibration)
- **Pattern:** Confidence catches up to low accuracy over time

**What/Where domains (object/spatial memory) trajectories:**
- **Early (T1):** UNDERCONFIDENT (~-0.25) - confidence lags accuracy
- **Late (T4):** OVERCONFIDENT (~+0.10) - accuracy declines faster
- **Change:** Δ ≈ +0.33 (WORSENING calibration)
- **Pattern:** Confidence maintained despite accuracy decline

### Mechanistic Explanation

**When Domain - Why Overconfident Early:**
- **Temporal compression fluency:** Events separated by only hours/days feel temporally proximate
- Subjective experience: "That just happened, I should remember the order"
- Temporal cues create FALSE sense of accessibility
- Metacognitive monitoring misled by temporal proximity
- BUT: Actual temporal memory is POOR (floor-effect accuracy from RQ 6.3.1)

**When Domain - Why Underconfident Late:**
- Temporal compression cues DEGRADE rapidly (6-day retention interval)
- Events now feel temporally remote
- Confidence appropriately drops
- But accuracy was ALREADY low (no further room to decline)
- Result: Confidence "catches up" to chronically low accuracy
- Calibration IMPROVES (becomes more realistic)

**What/Where Domains - Why Underconfident Early:**
- Object (What) and spatial (Where) memories have moderate initial accuracy
- But confidence lags behind due to retrieval fluency being lower than accuracy
- Participants conservative in metacognitive judgments

**What/Where Domains - Why Overconfident Late:**
- **Residual familiarity (What):** Object representations maintain subjective fluency even as accuracy declines
- **Spatial landmark salience (Where):** Salient spatial features maintain confidence
- Accuracy declines FASTER than confidence
- Calibration WORSENS (confidence exceeds performance)

### Theoretical Framework

**Cue-Dependent Metacognitive Monitoring:**

1. **Temporal cues (When):**
   - HIGH availability early (temporal compression)
   - RAPID degradation over retention interval
   - SHORT temporal stability
   - Result: Steep confidence decline tracking cue loss

2. **Object cues (What):**
   - MODERATE availability early (familiarity-based)
   - SLOW degradation (residual fluency persists)
   - LONG temporal stability
   - Result: Maintained confidence despite accuracy decline

3. **Spatial cues (Where):**
   - MODERATE availability early (landmark-based)
   - SLOW degradation (salient landmarks persist)
   - LONG temporal stability
   - Result: Maintained confidence despite accuracy decline

**Key Insight:** Metacognitive monitoring is NOT based on veridical memory strength, but on CUE AVAILABILITY. Different memory domains have cues with different temporal stability profiles, leading to DISSOCIABLE calibration trajectories.

### Comparison to Accuracy Trajectories (Ch5 5.2.1)

**Accuracy Decline Rates:**
- When: STEEPEST decline (poorest domain)
- What/Where: PARALLEL, moderate decline

**Confidence Decline Rates (Ch6 6.3.1):**
- When: STEEPEST decline (matching accuracy pattern)
- What/Where: PARALLEL, moderate decline

**Calibration Trajectories (Ch6 6.3.2):**
- When: IMPROVING (overconfident→underconfident)
- What/Where: WORSENING (underconfident→overconfident)
- **CROSSOVER INTERACTION**

**Resolution:** Calibration dynamics emerge from DIFFERENTIAL rates of confidence vs accuracy decline. When domain shows MATCHED decline rates (both steep) → improving calibration. What/Where domains show MISMATCHED rates (confidence slower than accuracy) → worsening calibration.

### Clinical/Assessment Implications

1. **Time-dependent calibration assessment:**
   - Early retention: When domain overconfident, What/Where underconfident
   - Late retention: Pattern REVERSES
   - Cannot assume static calibration profiles

2. **Domain-specific metacognitive training:**
   - When domain: Need training to reduce EARLY overconfidence (recognize temporal compression illusion)
   - What/Where domains: Need training to maintain LATE realism (resist familiarity/landmark fluency)

3. **Theoretical parsimony:**
   - Single mechanism (cue-based monitoring) explains all three domain patterns
   - No need for domain-specific metacognitive "modules"
   - Supports cue-utilization framework (Koriat, 1997)
