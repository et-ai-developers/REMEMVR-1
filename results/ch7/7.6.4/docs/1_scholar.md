- **Claim Made:** "Predictor relationships will weaken after purification"
- **Scholarly Criticism:** This assumes a unidirectional relationship where purification always weakens predictors. However, purification could potentially strengthen some predictor relationships if removed items were adding noise to individual differences measurement.
- **Counterevidence:** Psychometric theory suggests that removing poorly performing items should improve measurement precision, which could enhance rather than weaken predictor relationships.
- **Strength:** MODERATE

#### Known Methodological Confounds (Unaddressed)

**1. Correlated Slope Estimates Between Passes**
- **Confound Description:** Pre- and post-purification slopes are derived from overlapping item sets, creating non-independence
- **How It Could Affect Results:** Standard regression comparison tests assume independence; correlated slopes violate this assumption
- **Literature Evidence:** Dependent samples t-tests or paired regression analyses required when comparing related estimates
- **Why Relevant to This RQ:** Both slope estimates come from the same participants on overlapping item sets
- **Strength:** CRITICAL
- **Suggested Mitigation:** "Modify Step 3 - use dependent samples tests for slope differences, not independent comparisons. Consider structural equation modeling to account for slope correlation."

**2. Restriction of Range in Cognitive Predictors**
- **Confound Description:** N=100 healthy adults may show restricted range in cognitive test scores, attenuating correlations
- **How It Could Affect Results:** Range restriction could make predictor relationships appear weaker than in general population
- **Literature Evidence:** Range restriction is a well-known threat to validity in correlation studies
- **Why Relevant to This RQ:** Healthy volunteer samples often show restricted cognitive variability
- **Strength:** MODERATE
