#### Commission Errors (Critiques of Claims Made)

**1. Oversimplified Sleep-Memory Mechanism**
- **Location:** Section 2: Theoretical Background - "Sleep deprivation impairs memory retrieval acutely through disrupted hippocampal function and attention networks"
- **Claim Made:** Sleep affects memory through general hippocampal and attention disruption
- **Scholarly Criticism:** This characterization may oversimplify sleep-memory relationships, which involve complex interactions between sleep stages, consolidation timing, and specific memory systems
- **Strength:** MODERATE
- **Suggested Rebuttal:** "Acknowledge complexity while noting that acute sleep effects (focus of this RQ) operate primarily through general attention/executive mechanisms rather than consolidation-specific pathways, making the simplified characterization appropriate for same-test performance analysis."

**2. Causal Inference Claims May Be Overstated**
- **Location:** Section 1: Research Question - "allows causal inference about acute sleep impacts"
- **Claim Made:** Within-person design enables causal inference
- **Scholarly Criticism:** While within-person design controls for individual differences, it doesn't eliminate all confounding variables that could affect both sleep and next-day memory performance
- **Strength:** MINOR
- **Suggested Rebuttal:** "Clarify that design enables stronger causal inference than between-person correlations while acknowledging that unmeasured time-varying confounds (stress, health fluctuations) could still influence both sleep and memory."

#### Alternative Theoretical Frameworks (Not Considered)

**1. Circadian Rhythm Effects Not Addressed**
- **Alternative Theory:** Observed sleep-memory correlations might reflect underlying circadian rhythm differences rather than direct sleep effects
- **How It Applies:** Participants who naturally test at non-optimal circadian times might report poor sleep AND perform poorly due to circadian misalignment, not causal sleep effects
- **Why Concept.md Should Address It:** Time-of-day testing could confound sleep-memory relationships
- **Strength:** MODERATE
- **Suggested Acknowledgment:** "Add to Limitations - acknowledge that testing time wasn't standardized, so circadian effects could contribute to observed sleep-performance correlations."

**2. Mood-Mediated Sleep-Memory Relationships**
- **Alternative Theory:** Sleep affects mood, and mood affects memory performance, creating indirect rather than direct sleep-memory effects
- **How It Applies:** Poor sleep -> negative mood -> reduced motivation/attention -> poor memory performance
- **Why Concept.md Should Address It:** Distinguishes direct cognitive effects from mood-mediated effects
- **Strength:** MINOR
- **Suggested Acknowledgment:** "Note that analysis focuses on total sleep effects (including mood pathways) rather than isolating direct cognitive mechanisms."

**1. Subjective Sleep Quality Measurement Bias**
- **Confound Description:** Self-reported sleep quality may be influenced by memory confidence or performance expectations
- **How It Could Affect Results:** Participants who performed poorly might retrospectively rate their sleep as poor, creating spurious correlations
- **Why Relevant to This RQ:** Sleep quality is subjective Likert scale, susceptible to recall bias
- **Strength:** MODERATE
- **Suggested Mitigation:** "Acknowledge in Limitations that sleep quality is subjective and could be influenced by performance expectations. Note that sleep hours provides more objective complement."

**2. Uncontrolled External Factors**
- **Confound Description:** External stressors, health fluctuations, or life events could affect both sleep and memory on same day
- **How It Could Affect Results:** Common third variables could create spurious within-person correlations
- **Why Relevant to This RQ:** 6-day testing window allows multiple external influences
- **Strength:** MINOR
