### Theoretical Contextualization

**Metacognitive Monitoring and Memory Consolidation Independence:**

The null finding suggests that metacognitive skill (calibration accuracy) operates independently from memory consolidation stability:

1. **Separate Systems Hypothesis:**
   - Calibration reflects metacognitive monitoring processes (frontal cortex, executive function)
   - Trajectory stability reflects consolidation mechanisms (hippocampus, sleep-dependent consolidation)
   - These may be neurally and functionally dissociable systems

2. **Encoding Quality ` Consolidation Stability:**
   - Good calibration indicates accurate *assessment* of memory trace strength
   - But does NOT necessarily indicate *consistent* consolidation processes
   - A person can accurately judge "I remember this well" while still showing variable forgetting rates across items

3. **Trial-Level vs Aggregate-Level Processes:**
   - Calibration measured at trial level (item-by-item confidence-accuracy alignment)
   - Trajectory variability measured at aggregate level (SD across 4 timepoints)
   - These different levels of analysis may tap distinct cognitive processes

## 4. Limitations

### Sample Limitations

**Sample Size and Power:**
- N = 100 provides adequate power (0.80) for medium effects (r e 0.30) but underpowered for small effects (r = 0.20, power H 0.50)
- Observed r = 0.020 is far below small effect threshold, so power limitation unlikely explanation for null result
- However, cannot rule out very small true effect (r = 0.05-0.10) that study was underpowered to detect

**Demographic Constraints:**
- University undergraduate sample (age: M H 20-22) limits generalizability to older adults
- Older adults may show different calibration-stability relationships due to age-related changes in metacognition and consolidation
- Restricted education range (all current college students) prevents examining education effects

**Missing Data:**
- Zero missing data for this RQ (100/100 participants with complete calibration + trajectories)
- However, inherited any exclusions from dependency RQs (6.2.1 and Ch5 5.1.1)
- If participants with extreme calibration/variability were excluded earlier, could bias null result

### Methodological Limitations

**Measurement Issues:**

1. **Calibration Operationalization:**
   - Day 0 calibration defined as confidence_theta - accuracy_theta (z-standardized)
   - Single timepoint snapshot may not capture stable trait calibration
   - Calibration may vary across test sessions (RQ 6.2.1 examined this but current RQ used only Day 0)
   - Alternative: Aggregate calibration across all 4 sessions might be more reliable predictor

2. **Trajectory Variability Operationalization:**
   - Variability computed as SD of residuals (4 timepoints per participant)
   - Only 4 timepoints may underestimate true trajectory variability (limited sampling)
   - SD assumes residuals are independent, but serial autocorrelation possible (Days 0, 1, 3, 6 not equally spaced)
   - Alternative: Model-based variability (random slope variance) might capture stability differently

3. **Omnibus Factor Only:**
   - Analysis used aggregated "All" factor (What/Where/When combined)
   - Domain-specific calibration-stability relationships not tested
   - Possible that spatial calibration predicts spatial stability but not other domains (averaging could obscure)

**Design Constraints:**

1. **Cross-Sectional Correlation:**
   - Cannot infer causality (even if correlation were significant)
   - Calibration � stability direction assumed but not tested
   - Alternative explanation: Stability � calibration (stable forgetters develop better calibration over time)

2. **No Control for Confounds:**
   - No covariates included (age, sleep quality, cognitive ability)
   - Third variables could independently influence both calibration and stability
   - However, null result less vulnerable to confound criticism (no relationship to explain)

3. **Single Calibration Metric:**
   - Many calibration metrics exist (Goodman-Kruskal gamma, calibration curves, overconfidence bias)
   - This RQ used confidence-accuracy difference (z-standardized)
   - Different metrics might show different relationships with stability

**Statistical Limitations:**

1. **Pearson Correlation Assumptions:**
   - Assumes linear relationship (non-linear relationships not tested)
   - Assumes bivariate normality (not formally tested, though scatterplot suggests no severe violations)
   - Sensitive to outliers (though scatterplot shows no extreme outliers)

2. **Limited to Correlation:**
   - No regression model with covariates
   - No exploration of non-linear relationships (quadratic, threshold effects)
   - No subgroup analyses (fast vs slow forgetters, high vs low calibration groups)

3. **Multiple Comparisons:**
   - This RQ is part of Ch6 predictive analysis series (multiple tests of calibration relationships)
   - No family-wise error correction across Ch6 RQs
   - However, null result not affected by Type I error concerns

### Generalizability Constraints

**Population Generalizability:**

Findings may not generalize to:
- **Older adults:** Age-related changes in metacognition (calibration often declines with age) and consolidation (sleep disruption, hippocampal atrophy) may create calibration-stability correlations absent in young adults
- **Clinical populations:** MCI, dementia, or sleep disorder patients may show coupling between metacognitive deficits and consolidation instability
- **Children/adolescents:** Developing metacognitive and consolidation systems may show different relationships

**Context Generalizability:**

- **VR-specific:** Findings specific to desktop VR episodic memory task
- **May not generalize to:** Real-world episodic memories, verbal list learning, or fully immersive HMD VR
- **Task characteristics:** Structured encoding (10-minute VR session) may differ from naturalistic episodic encoding

**Construct Generalizability:**

- **Trajectory stability:** Operationalized as SD of residuals from power law LMM
- **May differ if:** Using different trajectory model (logarithmic, exponential), different timepoints (more sessions), or different stability metric (autocorrelation, intraclass correlation)

### Technical Limitations

**Dependency on Ch5 5.1.1 Model Selection:**

- Residuals extracted from "best" LMM (power law alpha=0.4) selected in Ch5 5.1.1
- Trajectory variability computed from THOSE residuals
- If different model were "best" (e.g., logarithmic), residuals and variability would differ
- Null finding robust across model choices would strengthen conclusion (not tested)

**TSVR Time Variable (Decision D070):**

- Residuals computed from model using TSVR (hours since encoding)
- Assumes continuous forgetting process
- May not capture discrete consolidation phases (sleep-dependent vs wake-dependent)
- Alternative: Nominal days might show different variability patterns

**Z-Standardization of Calibration:**

- Calibration z-standardized in RQ 6.2.1 (mean = 0, SD = 1)
- Removes absolute calibration level information (all participants centered)
- Focuses analysis on relative calibration (better vs worse than average)
- Absolute calibration-stability relationship not tested

### Null Result Interpretation Limitations

**Cannot Prove Absence of Effect:**

- Null result (p = 0.847, r = 0.020) suggests no meaningful relationship
- But cannot definitively prove zero correlation
- Confidence interval for r (not reported) would quantify uncertainty
- Equivalence testing would formally test "negligible effect" hypothesis

**Publication Bias Concerns:**

- Null results less likely to be published (file-drawer problem)
- This summary transparently reports null finding per Decision D068 philosophy
- Encourages publication to prevent selective reporting bias in literature
